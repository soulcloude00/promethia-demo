The Program

Four weeks. Four ships. One stage.

Here's how Promethia turns a summer into a body of work — the skill pillars we train, the two age tracks, the daily and weekly rhythm, and the arc from first build to Demo Day.

Skill pillars

What your child will actually get better at.

Every project is designed to train several pillars at once. We track growth through artifacts and observations — never marks.

Build & Make

From cardboard to code. Kids learn to turn an idea into a working thing using real tools — hand tools, simple electronics, block and text coding, and lots of tape.

  • Sketch → prototype → test → v2
  • Safe use of tools and materials
  • Simple circuits, motors and sensors
  • Coding games and interactive stories

They walk away with: A portfolio of working builds and the habit of finishing.

Public Speaking & Storytelling

We train speaking like a sport: little and often. Daily show-and-tells, story circles, improv games and pitch drills build up to a confident Demo Day talk.

  • Structure: hook, problem, demo, ask
  • Voice, pace, eye contact and pauses
  • Handling questions without panic
  • Telling a true story well

They walk away with: The confidence to stand up and be understood.

Problem Solving & Critical Thinking

Kids learn to find the real problem before solving it — by observing, asking, measuring and testing their assumptions.

  • Asking “why?” five times
  • Breaking big problems into small ones
  • Fair tests and simple experiments
  • Spotting assumptions and weak arguments

They walk away with: A repeatable way to attack any problem.

Mentoring & Being Mentored

Every camper is mentored by working professionals — and every camper mentors someone too. Teaching is the fastest way to learn.

  • Asking for help with a good question
  • Giving kind, specific feedback
  • Senior Buddies coach Juniors
  • Reflecting on what you learned

They walk away with: Knowing how to learn from people — and lift others up.

Teamwork & Leadership

Projects run in small teams with rotating roles — builder, tester, storyteller, timekeeper — so every child practises leading and following.

  • Dividing work and meeting deadlines
  • Disagreeing respectfully
  • Running a stand-up and a retro
  • Celebrating others' wins

They walk away with: The ability to make great things with other people.

Entrepreneurship & Creativity

Seniors run mini-ventures; Juniors open a pop-up “maker market.” Creativity is treated as a skill to practise, not a talent you're born with.

  • Who is it for? What do they need?
  • Pricing, simple budgets and trade-offs
  • Brainstorming without judgement
  • Pitching an idea in 60 seconds

They walk away with: Original ideas — and the courage to put them into the world.

Two age tracks

Built for how kids learn at each age.

Grades 1–5 · Ages ~6–10

Curiosity Makers

Play-first, hands-first. Juniors explore materials, build simple machines, tell stories and discover that their ideas are worth sharing.

  • Format: half-day, Mon–Fri (sample 9:30 am – 1:00 pm)
  • Group size: small pods with a dedicated camp guide
  • Speaking: show-and-tells, story circles, puppet pitches
  • Building: cardboard, paper circuits, simple machines, art-tech
  • Mentoring: paired with a Senior Buddy for weekly builds
Grades 6–10 · Ages ~11–16

Build & Pitch

Venture-style and ambitious. Seniors work on real problems with real users, build serious prototypes and pitch them to a panel.

  • Format: full day, Mon–Fri (sample 9:30 am – 3:30 pm)
  • Teams: 3–4 campers with rotating roles
  • Speaking: pitch drills, debates, interviews, a stage pitch
  • Building: rocketry, electronics, coding, game design, prototyping
  • Mentoring: pro mentor check-ins + coaching a Junior pod

The rhythm

Every day: Wall → Story → Explore → Make → Show.

Predictable structure makes kids brave. They always know what's coming, and they always end with something to show.

  • Wall — We gather at the Artifact Wall to review yesterday's ships and set today's goal.
  • Story — A short true story (an inventor, a failure, a founder) frames the challenge.
  • Explore — Investigate: take apart, observe, interview, test a hunch.
  • Make — The heart of the day. Build, break, fix, ask a mentor, build again.
  • Show — Everyone presents. Peers give one “glow” and one “grow.”

Beyond summer

The weekly school program.

Week by week

The summer arc.

Each week has a theme, a weekly ask, and a ship for each track — building up to Demo Day.

Draft outline. This week-by-week arc is a high-level draft and will be replaced by the finalised Summer 2027 curriculum. Themes, projects and order may change.
  1. Week1

    Make it move

    Physics, play and first ships. Kids get comfortable with tools, teammates and the stage.

    Weekly askBuild something that flies, rolls or launches — and make it go further by Friday.
    Juniors shipPaper planes → balloon cars → straw rockets; a marble run for the classroom
    Seniors shipWater-bottle rockets with fins & nose cones; measured launches and v2/v3 iterations
    Speaking focus60-second show-and-tell: “What I built and what broke”
  2. Week2

    Make it for someone

    Empathy and problem finding. Kids leave the studio to meet real people with real problems.

    Weekly askInterview someone in the neighbourhood and build something that makes their day easier.
    Juniors shipA helper gadget for a family member (e.g. a reminder box, a toy organiser)
    Seniors shipUser interviews in the community; paper-prototype “app” or physical prototype tested with the real user
    Speaking focusStory of a user: who we met, what we learned, what we changed
  3. Week3

    Make it together

    Systems, teams and code. Bigger, more ambitious team builds with rotating roles.

    Weekly askAs a team, build a game or machine that strangers can use without instructions.
    Juniors shipA cardboard arcade with games for younger kids; a team comic or picture book
    Seniors shipA digital or board game, or a sensor-driven gadget; public playtests and a team retro
    Speaking focusTeam pitch practice; giving and receiving feedback
  4. Week4

    Make it count

    Capstone and Demo Day. Kids pick their best idea, polish it and present it to the world.

    Weekly askShip your best project yet and present it on Demo Day.
    Juniors shipCapstone build + expo table; a short show-and-tell for families
    Seniors shipCapstone mini-venture + 3-minute stage pitch with Q&A from a guest panel
    Speaking focusDemo Day: every child presents

How we track growth

A report card you can hold.

The Artifact Wall

Every ship goes on the Wall with the camper's name, version number and one sentence on what they learned.

Builder's logbook

Each child keeps a sketchbook of ideas, failures, fixes and reflections — theirs to take home.

End-of-summer portfolio

Families receive a summary of everything their child shipped, with mentor notes on growth across the pillars.
