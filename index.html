Why Promethia

Read our philosophy

Personalized Guidance

Small squads of 4–6, one adult for every 8–12 kids, and near-peer mentors who remember your child's name and their last build.

Verified & Trusted

Background-verified staff, a POCSO-aligned child-protection policy, a two-adult rule and consent-first photos.

End-to-End Support

Curriculum, materials, facilitators, showcases and parent updates. Schools and families don't have to assemble anything.

Growth Mindset

Progress over polish. Awards go to the biggest leap, the best comeback and the most helpful mentor, never to the shiniest project.

How it works

Wall → Story → Explore → Make → Show.

Every Promethia session follows the same five beats, so kids always know what's coming and always finish with something to show. Timings shown for a 90-minute session.

  1. Wall

    Start at the Artifact Wall: what did we ship, what broke, what's next?

    ~10 min

  2. Story

    A short true story (an inventor, a failure, a local hero) frames the challenge.

    ~10 min

  3. Explore

    Poke, test, take apart, interview. Find the real problem before building.

    ~20 min

  4. Make

    The longest block. Sketch, prototype, break it, fix it, ask a mentor.

    ~40 min

  5. Show

    Every child presents. Peers give one glow and one grow.

    ~10 min

And every week

  1. Mon

    Spark

    The weekly ask drops. One driving question, one goal card on the wall.

  2. Tue

    Explore

    Go find out. Interviews, observation and experiments with real people.

  3. Wed

    Build

    Make version one: fast, rough and gloriously imperfect.

  4. Thu

    Feedback

    Critique day. Kind, specific, helpful notes, then version two.

  5. Fri

    Ship

    Projects go on the Wall. Everyone presents. Parents get a note home.

Every term ends on a real stage: Demo Day.

Families, mentors and guest judges from Bangalore's builder community. Juniors run expo booths; seniors pitch and take live questions.

Our story

The Fire of Knowledge

In the Greek myth, the Titan Prometheus carried fire down from Olympus and gave it to people. Not a finished gift, but a spark: the power to make tools, light the dark and build what didn't exist yet.

That is our name and our whole idea. We don't hand children answers. We hand them the spark (a problem, a few materials, a mentor beside them) and stand back while they build, test and tell the world what they made.

“Fire isn't something you're given. It's something you learn to carry.”
