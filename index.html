How it works

Recap → Story → Explore → Make → Show.

Every session follows the same five beats, so students know what's coming and always finish with something to show.

  1. Recap

    Look back at last session: what worked, what didn't, what's next?

    ~5 min

  2. Story

    A short true story (an inventor, a failure, a local hero) frames the challenge.

    ~10 min

  3. Explore

    Ask questions, interview a classmate, find the real problem first.

    ~20 min

  4. Make

    The longest block. Prompt, sketch, design, test it on a classmate, improve it.

    ~40 min

  5. Show

    Every child presents. Peers give one glow and one grow.

    ~10 min

Every cohort ends on a real stage.

The pilot closes with a showcase in early December. Students present their work to families and teachers, and take questions.

Our story

The Fire of Knowledge

In the Greek myth, the Titan Prometheus carried fire down from Olympus and gave it to people. Not a finished gift, but a spark: the power to make tools, light the dark and build what didn't exist yet.

That is our name and our whole idea. We don't hand children answers. We hand them the spark (a problem, a few materials, a mentor beside them) and stand back while they build, test and tell the world what they made.

“Fire isn't something you're given. It's something you learn to carry.”
