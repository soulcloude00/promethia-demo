How it works

From lab game to stage

A short, focused pilot. Here's the whole journey.

  1. 01

    Early Nov · 1 lab period

    Play Mind Expedition

    A short browser game in the school computer lab. Around 300 students play; with teacher input, 30–50 are invited. It's not an exam or a rank.

  2. 02

    Nov · 60–90 min each

    Join 5–7 sessions

    Speaking, supervised AI prompting and design, in school slots on weekdays.

  3. 03

    Every week

    Parents see progress

    A short strengths-based scorecard after each week: what went well, the evidence, one next step.

  4. 04

    Early December

    Showcase

    Students present their work to families and teachers and take questions.

Why "Promethia"

The fire of knowledge

In the Greek myth, Prometheus carried fire to people: not a finished gift, but a spark to make tools and light the dark.

That's our idea. We don't hand children answers. We give them a challenge, the right tools and a stage, then step back while they make something and explain it.

FAQ

Questions, answered

Who is the pilot for?

Students in Grades 5–8 at a partner school in Bangalore. 30–50 students join the first cohort.

How are students chosen?

Students play Mind Expedition, a short game in the school computer lab. Together with teacher input, it helps form a manageable group. It isn't an IQ test and nobody is ranked.

When does it run?

Shortlisting in the first week of November 2026, 5–7 weekday sessions through November, and a showcase in early December.

What does it cost?

Mostly free. At most, families cover materials. Some cohorts may use a small refundable deposit to hold a seat.

Do children use AI on their own?

No. AI is used only through a supervised Class Terminal run by the facilitator. Students don't create accounts.

Can my school host a cohort?

Yes. We're talking with partner schools in Bangalore about cohorts in 2027. See the page for schools.
