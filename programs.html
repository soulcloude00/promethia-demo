The wider curriculum

Eight domains we'll grow into.

The pilot focuses on three of these. The rest join the weekly programme over time, each with a younger and an older version.

Human Intelligence

The durable human skills no machine replaces. Woven into every session, and the focus of its own weekly modules.

  • Communication & active listening
  • Critical thinking
  • Collaboration
  • Leadership & responsibility
Younger studentsListening circles, turn-taking team games, “why do you think that?” talks and fair-play discussions.
Older studentsStructured debates, spotting weak arguments, running a team stand-up and retro, giving kind and specific feedback, leading a junior squad.

Artificial Intelligence (AI)

What AI is, how it learns from examples, where it goes wrong, and how to use it thoughtfully. Unplugged first, always supervised.

  • How machines learn from examples
  • Data, patterns and predictions
  • Checking AI answers
  • Using AI responsibly
Younger studentsMostly unplugged: sorting and pattern games, “teach the robot” role-play, spotting AI in everyday life.
Older studentsTrain a simple image or sound classifier with a beginner-friendly tool, test it for mistakes and bias, fact-check AI answers, debate responsible use.

Robotics

Design, build, code and debug machines that sense and move, from cardboard bots to microcontroller robots.

  • Mechanisms, motors & sensors
  • Sequencing and block / text coding
  • Debugging step by step
  • Team engineering
Younger studentsCardboard bots with simple motors, “program your friend” sequencing games, block-coded movement.
Older studentsMicrocontroller robots with sensors and motors (line-followers, obstacle-avoiders, simple arms), designed and debugged in teams. Uses the school's ATL kit where one exists.

Building & Making

Turning an idea into a working thing with real tools and materials, and then making version two.

  • Sketch → prototype → test → v2
  • Safe use of tools and materials
  • Measuring and joining
  • Finishing what you start
Younger studentsCardboard, clay, paper circuits, simple machines, straw rockets and marble runs.
Older studentsWater-bottle rocketry, engineering challenges under constraints, functional prototypes with real users.

Design

Building for real people: noticing needs, sketching many ideas, prototyping and testing with users.

  • Observing and empathising
  • Sketching many ideas
  • Prototyping fast
  • Testing with real users
Younger studentsDraw it before you build it; design a helper gadget for someone at home or in class.
Older studentsDesign sprints, user interviews and paper or clickable prototypes tested with five or more real users.

Public Speaking & Storytelling

Speaking practised like a sport, little and often, so presenting becomes normal, not scary.

  • Structure: hook, problem, demo, ask
  • Voice, pace and eye contact
  • Handling questions
  • Telling a true story well
Younger studentsShow-and-tells, story circles, puppet pitches and a first taste of the stage.
Older studentsTable topics, organised 1–2-minute talks, a 90-second demo and a 2–3-minute pitch with live Q&A.

Problem Solving

Finding the real problem before solving it, by observing, asking, measuring and testing assumptions.

  • Asking “why?” five times
  • Breaking big problems into small ones
  • Fair tests and experiments
  • Learning from what broke
Younger studentsPuzzles, fair-test experiments and “what happens if…?” investigations.
Older studentsConstraint challenges, root-cause hunts and experiments with a written conclusion and trade-off.

Entrepreneurship

Who is it for, what do they need, what is it worth? Kids learn to turn ideas into value for real people.

  • Spotting needs
  • Simple pricing and budgets
  • Pitching an idea
  • Trade-offs and decisions
Younger studentsA pop-up maker market with play coins: make it, price it, sell it.
Older studentsMini-ventures with simple budgets, a ≤6-slide pitch deck and a pitch to a guest panel.

Activities

Rockets before lunch. Pitches after.

The activity library we draw from as the programme grows. Each one ends with something real to show.

  • Build · All grades

    Build workshops

    Hands-on maker sessions with cardboard, wood, fabric, clay and recycled materials. Measure, cut, join, iterate.

    Ships: A working physical prototype

  • Build · All grades

    Rocketry & launch day

    From straw rockets to water-bottle rockets with fins and nose cones. Measure, launch, adjust, relaunch.

    Ships: A rocket that flies further every version

  • Build · Think · All grades

    Engineering challenges

    Bridges, towers, egg drops and marble runs under constraints. Physics made physical.

    Ships: A structure tested to its limit

  • Tech · All grades

    Circuits & electronics

    Copper-tape paper circuits for juniors; breadboards, sensors and motors for seniors.

    Ships: A gadget that senses or responds

  • Tech · All grades

    Coding & game-making

    Block coding for juniors, beginner text coding for seniors. Games, animations, interactive stories.

    Ships: A playable game or animation

  • Tech · Build · All grades

    Robotics lab

    Cardboard bots and “program your friend” games for juniors; line-followers and obstacle-avoiders for seniors.

    Ships: A robot that senses and moves

  • Tech · Think · All grades

    AI explorers

    Unplugged sorting and pattern games for juniors; seniors train a simple classifier, test where it fails, fact-check AI answers.

    Ships: A mini-model and an honest “what it gets wrong” report

  • Design · Older students

    Design sprints

    How product teams work, compressed: understand, sketch, decide, prototype, test. In days, not months.

    Ships: A tested paper or clickable prototype

  • Think · Explore · Older students

    User interviews

    Ask about past behaviour, not hypotheticals. Listen hard, spot real problems, then go and talk to real people.

    Ships: Interview notes and a “How might we…?”

  • Speak · All grades

    Public speaking lab

    Warm-ups, voice games, table topics and daily show-and-tells. Speaking practised like a sport.

    Ships: A confident 1–3 minute talk

  • Speak · All grades

    Storytelling studio

    Story spines, comic-making and personal narrative: start strong, build tension, land the ending.

    Ships: A told, drawn or recorded story

  • Speak · Team · Older students

    Pitch practice

    Problem → users and evidence → solution → value → ask. Rehearse, get feedback, pitch to a guest panel.

    Ships: A pitch deck and a live pitch

  • Think · Team · All grades

    Maker market

    Juniors run a pop-up market with play coins; seniors run mini-ventures with simple budgets.

    Ships: A product, a price and a customer

  • Team · All grades

    Team challenges

    Timed team missions: escape-room puzzles, build-offs and relays with rotating roles.

    Ships: A team win (and a team retro)

  • Team · All grades

    Near-peer mentoring

    Trained older-student mentors coach junior squads, hands behind their backs, with an adult always in the room.

    Ships: A mentor log and a mentee who did it themselves

  • Explore · All grades

    Field expeditions

    Maker spaces, markets, workshops and green spaces around Bangalore: observe, interview, collect problems.

    Ships: A field journal full of problems to solve

  • Explore · Think · All grades

    Curiosity experiments

    Kitchen chemistry, fair tests and “what happens if…?” investigations. Guess, test, measure, explain.

    Ships: An experiment with a conclusion

  • Speak · Tech · Older students

    Podcast & video lab

    Plan, record and edit a short episode interviewing a mentor, a shopkeeper or a grandparent.

    Ships: An episode shared with family

  • Create · All grades

    Invention hour

    Open-ended free build. Wild ideas protected. The only rule: show it at the end.

    Ships: Something nobody asked for (the best kind)

  • Show · All grades

    Demo Day

    The finale: expo booths for juniors, stage pitches for seniors, families and guest judges in the audience.

    Ships: Your best work, shown to the world
