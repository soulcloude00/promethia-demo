For Students

Make something real. Then own the stage.

At Promethia you don't sit and listen. You build things out of cardboard, circuits and code, test them on real people, fix what breaks, and present your work every Friday. By Demo Day, you'll have a body of work and a voice to match.

On the Wall

What you'll do

Not a class. A workshop with a stage.

Six things you'll do every week, whichever track you're in.

Build real things

Marble runs, wobble-bots, light-up cards, robots, apps on paper. Real tools, real materials, and a version two of everything.

Speak every day

Show-and-tell, story spines and table topics. Little and often, until the mic feels normal.

Solve real problems

Interview your neighbour, your bus driver, your grandmother. Find what's actually annoying them, and fix it.

Work in a squad

Rotate roles (builder, tester, storyteller, timekeeper) so you learn to lead and to back someone up.

Mentor and be mentored

Older students coach younger squads. Guest engineers and founders drop in to give feedback.

Explore AI & robotics

Train a mini-model, catch it making mistakes, wire a robot that senses and moves.

The build-and-ship loop

Every week, you ship.

One question. Five days. One thing you made, on the Wall, with your name on it. Skip a Friday? We don't do that.

The Artifact Wall is the report card. No marks, no ranks. Your builds, versions, interview notes and talk videos are the evidence of how far you've come.

  1. Spark

    Monday's question drops. Write your goal on a card and pin it to the Wall.

  2. Find out

    Go and talk to real people. Ask what happened last time, not “would you use my idea?”

  3. Build v1

    Make the roughest version that shows your idea. Ugly is allowed. Unfinished is not.

  4. Get feedback

    Thursday is critique day: “I like how…”, “I wonder if…”, “What if you tried…”

  5. Ship it

    Friday: your work goes on the Wall and you show it. Seniors post a weekly update too.

Age tracks

Built for how you learn, at your age.

Grades 1–5 · ages ~6–10

Makers

Play-first, hands-first. Short build blocks, lots of movement, and the joy of finding out your idea is worth sharing.

You'll build
Cardboard marble runs, cup-and-straw spinners, light-up cards, a toy for someone younger
You'll say
30-second show-and-tells → a 60–90-second demo with your project in hand
Your squad
4–5 friends, a peer buddy, and an older mentor from Week 3
Screens
None or almost none. Your hands are the main tool.
Grades 6–10 · ages ~11–16

Builders

Venture-style and ambitious. You pick one real problem and carry it from idea to working version to a pitch in front of a panel.

You'll build
Mechanisms, circuits and sensors, a microcontroller robot, a game or a no-code app, or a non-tech product or service
You'll say
Daily table topics → a 90-second demo + a 2–3-minute pitch with live Q&A
Your team
A studio team of 4–6. You own your project; the team makes it better.
Lead
Grade 8–10s can train as near-peer mentors and coach a junior squad.

Demo Day

The last Friday. Your stage.

Families, mentors and guest judges fill the room. Juniors guide visitors around their booths and give a 60–90-second demo with their project in hand. Seniors show a 90-second demo and pitch for 2–3 minutes to a panel, then take questions.

  • Nobody presents alone if they don't want to: squads and pairs are welcome.
  • Talk in English, Kannada, Hindi or a mix. Clarity counts, not accent.
  • Every child gets an “I Can” certificate naming a real skill they gained.

“What can you build now that you couldn't six weeks ago?”
The closing-circle question

Awards for growth, not polish

  • Biggest Leap. The most growth between your Day-1 and final-week videos, and between v1 and your final build.
  • Best Comeback. The clearest proof that feedback actually changed what you made.
  • Best Interview Insight. The most surprising thing you learned from a real person.
  • Best Mentor / Helper. Voted by your peers, for making others better.
  • Audience Choice. Families vote with tokens at the booths.

Juniors are never ranked. Everyone is celebrated.
